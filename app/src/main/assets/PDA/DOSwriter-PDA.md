# How to Use the The DOSwriter PDA Lifestyle Management System

Having always used lists to keep track of personal tasks, I improved on the basic linear list format with this handy "PDA" that you can carry around in your pocket. PDA stands for Person Digital Analog. It's digital because you  maintain it on your computer. It's analog because you print it out when it gets used up or misplaced.

DOSwriter transforms your digital task list into a physical, pocket-sized "Personal Digital Assistant" (PDA). By using the **8-in-1 Mini-book** export, you can carry your active projects with you wherever you go.

## Feedback Helps Your Sense of Accomplishment

The salient features of the DOSwriter PDA are 1) it folds into a handy, readable pocket list and 2) it provides a means to make small contributions to ongoing self-improvement goals.

The latter is crucial to making progress in those areas you want to grow such as entrepreneurship, physical fitness, personal finance, learning, and travel preparation.

A linear list format gives a poor sense of progress. Some personal efforts are measured in years. To simply write a long-term has no feedback, the critical element to any improvement system. Another problem is that you always have things to do and once done they get deleted. You always face that interminable list but probably get no sense of accomplishment.

But if you have an ongoing space for your goals you can add feedback in the form of a simple hash mark next to each self-improvement activity. Now you instead write "pushups" and every day that you knock out a set you just put a hashmark in groups of 5. After one or two weeks when you open your list you see that. If you don't it gently reminds you to make some. Rather than an oppressive goal it prods you to do those daily increments that slowly up to results.

The ongoing personal tasks are still there but on a different page of your PDA. They will always be there and cannot be escaped. But this format moves them to a priority list that is easier to handle or puts them on a long-term list for continued management.


## Creating your PDA

1. Switch to **ToDo Mode** using `ALT + W`.
2. Populate Buffers 1 through 8 with your categories (e.g., Shopping, Work, Errands).
3. Export the file: Go to `PUBLISH (ALT + P) > 8-in-1 Minibook`.
4. Save the generated PDF and print it on a standard 8.5x11 or A4 sheet of paper.
