Gentlemen: I am very glad to have the opportunity of speaking a few words with you this evening. I have not often addressed gatherings of this kind, but I have always found them to be composed of thoughtful and attentive listeners, and therefore a pleasure to address.

There are many who suppose that speaking is a simple matter—merely standing up and saying what comes into one's head. But I have long been of the opinion that speaking well is one of the most difficult arts known to man. It requires not only that a man shall have something to say, but that he shall say it in such a way that his hearers will believe he knew it all the time.

For my own part, I have sometimes felt that I could make a better speech if I were allowed to go home and prepare it afterward. Yet the world insists upon having the speech first, and the preparation afterward.

However, I shall not detain you long. If I succeed in making myself understood, I shall be satisfied; if I fail, I shall at least have had the satisfaction of trying.
