LIFETIME FINANCIAL MANAGEMENT

I. The Economy In Which We Live And Our Daily Bread

1. The Industrial Economy 

!$P 1. As a family or as a single person, we are members of a national economy and society making our contributions to its performance, and being rewarded for our efforts according to the value the economy and society determine for them. The reward we get for our efforts as income is what we use to pay for our current needs and to provide for the future. As a family or as a single person, we live our lives as independent people, but the twists and turns the economy and society as a whole take over time work their influence on what fate has in store for us. The better we understand how the economy works and its interaction with our society, the better we will be at managing for today and planning for tomorrow. So let us look into the nature and motivation of the modern economy. 

!$P 2. In a society we live by what we produce. And in a world made up of national economies, we trade some of what we produce with other countries for what they produce. What we produce and what we get in trade with other countries is based on the demand we place on ourselves for privately produced goods and services as well as those goods and services we insist be produced by government. Individually, we exert a demand for some of those goods and services according to our unique preferences and the income we have to satisfy those preferences. 

!$P 3. A modern economy is based on money as a means for exchanging goods and services. There is a circular flow of activity in the production of goods and services in which workers of all kinds are compensated individually with money for what they do, representing their share of those goods and services available in the marketplaces of the economy. For any one individual, however, that income may be not only be in the form of wages and salary, but also in the form of dividends from ownership in producing firms, interest from loans made to others, payments by government, rent from property, and gifts from others. 

!$P 4. Wages or salary as a share of goods and services depend on the value of a person's labor in producing those goods and services; what that value might be is determined not only by the availability of labor skills, but by the demand of the public at large for the goods and services produced by those skills. The compensation of a highly talented professional athlete, a construction worker, a medical specialist, an office worker, a movie actor, and a computer programmer depends on their availability and the value the public at large puts on what they produce. For most walks of life, education plays an important role in the income paid to individuals for the work they do. So much of what the public wants today requires skills developed only through higher education. 

!$P 5. The need for those skills is traceable to successive scientific discoveries which have been exploited through investment in the means to produce goods and services that enhance our standard of living. The greater skills required to produce more valuable output are reflected in higher real wages and salaries which are transformed into a demand for the very goods and services they are instrumental in producing. So we can see if an economy is to enjoy increases in its standard of living there must be investment in new products and better ways to produce all products. Ther must also be increases in wages and salaries reflective of greater output, and a transformation of that income into demand for the greater output.
